No direct B9 use.

3. **arXiv 2609.15906: Authorization Architectures for Tool-Using AI Agents** ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15906)) — September paper laying out a principal hierarchy (user, operator, orchestrator, sub-agent, tool endpoint) and five layers: identity/credentials, delegation and scope propagation, runtime enforcement, prompt injection as authorization bypass, and auditability. **Applicable: YES** — A ready checklist for The Construct's credential-bearing skills and Hermes loops: scope what each sub-agent inherits and log it.

4. **Ema raises $77M Series B for 'AI employees' in enterprise HR, IT and finance** ([source](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/23/ema-raises-77m-in-funding-to-deploy-ai-employees-across-enterprise-hr-it-and-finance-departments/)) — Announced 23 Sep (Creagis lead, Accel returning): Ema's prebuilt agents plan and execute tasks inside existing enterprise systems and escalate sensitive actions to a human approver; it cites Wipro and Hitachi deployments. **Applicable: MAYBE** — Validates PRAGMA's packaged-operator pitch (prebuilt role agents, not DIY frameworks) and the approve-before-sensitive-action pattern The Construct already uses; no direct B9 use.

**PRAXIS lesson:** Three of today's four items are about the same thing, agents acting with borrowed authority. As model prices keep halving, the scarce layer for The Construct is scoping and auditing what each agent is allowed to do, not the model underneath it.
