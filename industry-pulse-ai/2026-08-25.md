3. **CORPUS GAP — a multi-agent paper reports >90% internal error interception by giving agents OPPOSING incentives, which is the control this report demonstrably lacks** ([arxiv.org/abs/2601.14351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14351)) — arXiv 2601.14351 (Vijayaraghavan, Jayachandran, Murthy, Govindan, Subramanian) proposes an "AI Office" architecture for making unreliable LLMs usable in production: teams of independent agents with strict role boundaries — planners, executors, critics, experts — sharing a common goal but holding deliberately OPPOSING incentives, coordinated through a remote code executor that keeps data transformation and tool invocation separate from the reasoning models. The reported result is over 90% of internal errors intercepted before user exposure, bought at a modest cost in action velocity. **Applicable: YES** — and the applicability is uncomfortable rather than aspirational. The Construct's roster is planners and executors: Keystone plans, Ledger models, Pulse and Lens and Mallard scan, Hermes executes. Nothing in the daily loop is incentivized to be wrong-footed by the others. The consequence is on the record in this very corpus — a false "no signage MCP server" negative survived weeks of restatement, and seven Claude outages in twenty-four days went unlogged, and neither was caught by the agent producing the report, because the agent producing the report has no adversary. `swarm-strategy-review` is the nearest thing we own, but it is invoked by hand for strategy calls, not wired into a scheduled job that ships to a dashboard daily. The cheap adoption is one role, not an org chart: a critic step inside Pulse whose only job is to attempt to FALSIFY each bullet's central claim before it ships — search for the thing itself, check the date, check whether the corpus already carries it — with a bullet that survives shipping and one that does not being dropped rather than softened. That is the same four-move discipline as yesterday's bullet 9, given teeth by an incentive instead of an intention. Note also the paper's second structural point, which lands on the Hermes design: keeping tool invocation and data transformation in a separate executor from the reasoning model is what makes role boundaries enforceable rather than merely stated.
