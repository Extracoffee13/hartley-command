3. **THE MISS, AND THE DAY'S REAL FINDING — Anthropic published the first complete computer-checked proof of Fermat's Last Theorem on September 4, squarely in window, and the morning fire did not see it, because it was published to `anthropic.com/research` — a FIFTH first-party surface that yesterday's freshly-corrected four-surface checklist does not name** ([anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem)) — Date read off the primary's own byline: `Sep 4, 2026`, corroborated by SiliconANGLE's article stamped `published_time: 2026-09-05T00:41:41+00:00` / `UPDATED 20:41 EDT / SEPTEMBER 04 2026`. Claude worked largely autonomously for 11 days, producing 13 million lines of Lean — over 5x the size of Mathlib, the community's principal proof library — and proving 30,300 theorems (29,500 used in the final proof), consuming roughly six billion output tokens from an internal research model Anthropic describes as "roughly comparable to Claude Fable 5.1." The mechanism is the part that matters here and it is not the mathematics. **Anthropic's first attempts FAILED for exactly the reason this fleet's multi-agent work fails:** in Anthropic's own words, "agents had some early success, [but] they quickly lost track of the project's state and stopped collaborating effectively," and those failed runs still contributed ~7% of the non-boilerplate lines in the final proof. The fix was not a better model. It was a shared external state object — Prove2Me, an open platform that maintains a directed acyclic graph of theorem statements that agents read to decide what to attempt next, separates statements from proofs into different files to cut compile cost, and keeps a natural-language description of each statement so agents can search and reuse instead of re-deriving. Human mathematical input was limited to occasional one-line steers ("Jacobian as a scheme sounds high priority"). Kevin Buzzard, who reviewed the proof, called it an "extraordinary autoformalization achievement" and said the artefacts "are now robust enough to be built upon." Full proof at [github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem). A footnote worth the fleet's attention: three researchers on personal Claude Max plans, collaborating only through Prove2Me, formalized Vinogradov's Three Primes Theorem in three days — so the scaffold, not the token budget, is the scarce input. **Applicable: YES** — and not for the mathematics. This is the largest published multi-agent run to date and its stated failure mode is memory degradation and loss of shared state across parallel agents, which is precisely the failure this corpus has been patching by hand with registries, `latest.json` files, praxis blocks and the "reconcile before building" rule. Anthropic's answer generalises: give the agents ONE external, machine-readable DAG of what is done, what is claimed, and what is next, and let each agent read its next task off that graph rather than off its own context. The Construct already has the raw material for this — the flywheel registries, the approval queue, `b9-knowledge-graph`'s typed entity/relation graph — but no single DAG that every agent reads before choosing work. Pair it with bullet 2: `ant apply` is how such a graph gets declared and version-controlled. No bearing on Brand 9 or PRAGMA directly, though the "AI produces the artefact, a machine verifies it, a human reads only the summary" pattern is the exact shape PRAGMA sells.
