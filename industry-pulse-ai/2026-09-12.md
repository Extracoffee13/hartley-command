1. **THE CHECK RAN AGAIN AND THIS TIME IT FOUND SOMETHING: ANTHROPIC NAMED SEVEN CHINA-BASED LABS THAT DISTILLED CLAUDE THROUGH FRAUDULENT ACCOUNTS AND PROXY NETWORKS — AND A SWEEP OF EVERY `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` AND MODEL-ROUTE IN BOBBY'S CONNECTED FOLDERS TURNED UP THREE OF THOSE NAMES, TWO INERT AND ONE LIVE** ([www.anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026)) — Anthropic published "Countering misuse of AI: September 2026" on Sep 10, covering activity disrupted December 2025–August 2026 across seven harm areas: cyber operations, influence operations, surveillance, scams and fraud, biological misuse, conventional weapons, and DISTILLATION. Headline cyber finding: in one case (GTG-50014, ShinyHunters affiliates) the operators took "over 2,100 Azure AD token sets spanning more than 40 corporate tenants in about 34 hours. AI agents performed nearly all of the work," with individual breaches completed in two to three hours; a Russian case (GTG-20006, tradecraft consistent with Midnight Blizzard) hit 20+ organizations and built a loop that detected when its own malware was flagged and rewrote it until it evaded detection; a Changsha-based group (GTG-10007) ran a standing fleet of 13 unattended collection agents and surfaced "more than a dozen possible zero day findings in a single month." Anthropic's own caveat is important and this report will not drop it: "humans have retained the decisions that matter most... target selection, monetization... and review of results," and "autonomy and harm are separate axes." Two items from the scams section land directly on operators like us: a network of fraudulent "cheap Claude" RESELLERS (GTG-50021) that silently proxied customer traffic and harvested credentials, and malware distributed under a spoofed Claude Code identity. And the distillation section names Alibaba/Qwen (>151M exchanges May–July, peak ~3M/day across 3,500+ fraudulent accounts), Moonshot (>23M, and ~300,000 of its OWN customers' live requests relayed through Claude in ten days), DeepSeek (>12.1M in 14 days), Zhipu/Z.ai (>3.4M, and it "gave up" on Fable then switched to Opus 4.6 "expressly because they assessed the safeguards were weaker"), Xiaomi, SenseTime and MiniMax (shell-company proxy network). Anthropic states its own systems were not compromised; the stolen keys came from customer environments. Source-quality note: vendor primary opened and read in full, not an aggregator summary; the actor attributions are Anthropic's own and are stated as consistent-with rather than confirmed. **Applicable: YES** — YES, and the check that follows from it is a grep, so this fire ran it before writing this sentence. THE SWEEP: every `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` / `ANTHROPIC_AUTH_TOKEN` / `ANTHROPIC_API_URL` occurrence and every Anthropic-or-Claude-shaped endpoint across .hermes, Atlas, Projects and AP-v2. THREE RESULTS, and the distinction between them is the entire finding. (i) TWO INERT: the vendored third-party skill repo `fcakyon/claude-codex-settings` (under .hermes/skills/hartley-construct/skills/, last commit May 29 2026) ships `settings-minimax.json` and `settings-zai.json`, which set `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` to `https://api.minimax.io/anthropic` and `https://api.z.ai/api/anthropic` and remap Opus/Sonnet/Haiku onto MiniMax-M2.7 and GLM-5-Turbo. Both are MiniMax and Zhipu/Z.ai — two of the seven labs named two days ago. THEY ARE NOT ACTIVE: the tokens are literal placeholders ("your_minimax_api_key"), nothing outside that repo references either file, and no ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL is set in ~/.hermes/.env. Verdict: PRESENT, NAMED, INERT — an unused profile, not a route. (ii) ONE LIVE: `~/.hermes/.env` contains `OPENROUTER_MODEL=deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash`. DeepSeek is the third name, and unlike the other two this line is real configuration in the executor's own environment file. The honest read is NOT "we were distilled" — that inverts the direction. The read is DATA EGRESS: any Hermes work that falls through to the OpenRouter path sends its context to DeepSeek, and Hermes is the arm holding the deploy SSH key, GSC and GitHub write access. That is a governance question about where our own context goes, and it is now answerable in one line instead of being unknown. (iii) CLEAN: 429 references to api.anthropic.com and no third-party Anthropic-shaped endpoint anywhere else; the only proxy hostnames present are documentation examples. THE OPERATOR RULE THIS EARNS, because the reseller case makes it concrete: never route Claude traffic through a discounted third-party endpoint, and treat any "cheap Claude" offer as a credential-harvesting funnel by default — Anthropic just documented that exact business model.

2. **MICROSOFT DOCUMENTED AN AI-WRITTEN EXECUTIVE-IMPERSONATION AND INVOICE-FRAUD CAMPAIGN REQUESTING ~$50,000 PAYMENTS FROM VENDORS THAT WERE NEVER COMPROMISED — THE FIRST ITEM IN WEEKS AIMED AT A BUSINESS SHAPED EXACTLY LIKE BRAND 9** ([www.microsoft.com](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/09/10/protecting-organizations-ai-assisted-executive-impersonation-invoice-fraud/)) — Microsoft published on Sep 10 a writeup of an August 3–5 campaign in which attackers impersonated company executives and submitted fraudulent invoices requesting payments of nearly $50,000, with signs of AI-assisted writing in the lures. The finding that makes it operational rather than interesting: Microsoft found NO EVIDENCE the impersonated vendors were compromised — the attacker did not need to break into anything, only to write convincingly as someone the recipient already trusts. Microsoft's stated mitigation is a process control, not a product: verify payment requests through a channel OUTSIDE the convincing-looking email thread. Source-quality note: vendor security-research primary; the direct URL was outside this session's fetch provenance set so the detail here is as carried by the AI Weekly daily edition of Sep 11 quoting it, and the campaign dates, amount and no-compromise finding should be re-read against the Microsoft post directly before anyone circulates the dollar figure. **Applicable: YES** — YES, and it is the most immediately actionable bullet on the board for the business that actually bills. Brand 9 is precisely the target profile: a small operation that sends and receives invoices, works under purchase orders from a very large counterparty (Lennar), and has a principal whose name, title, writing voice and email address are all public on brand9signs.com and LinkedIn. The attack needs nothing else. Two consequences, both cheap. FIRST, a payment-verification rule that exists as a written rule rather than as Bobby's instinct: any change to banking details, any new payee, and any invoice above a stated threshold gets confirmed by phone to a number already on file — NOT a number in the email, and not a reply to the thread. That is one paragraph and it belongs with the B9 operating docs, not in this report. SECOND, and this is the part that cuts toward the agent ecosystem: an AI-written lure defeats the tell most people rely on, which is bad grammar. Every "does this look legitimate" heuristic in the business is now degraded, INCLUDING the ones inside our own agents — an agent asked to triage B9 mail cannot use writing quality as a signal either. The control has to be the out-of-band callback, for humans and agents alike. Add it to the (a) incident-log schema as an eighth consideration: an agent that PROCESSES a payment request should record what channel verified it.

3. **CURSOR SHIPPED A COORDINATOR AGENT THAT PLANS, DELEGATES TO SUBAGENTS, HOLDS CONTEXT FOR MONTHS, RUNS ON A CLOUD MACHINE WITH THE LAPTOP CLOSED, AND WATCHES A SLACK CHANNEL OR A SCHEDULE FOR WORK TO PICK UP — WHICH IS THE CONSTRUCT'S MAESTRO ARCHITECTURE, SOLD** ([cursor.com](https://cursor.com/changelog/projects)) — Cursor launched Projects in beta on Sep 10. The design: a coordinator agent that "doesn't write code itself but directs other agents that do," and because it delegates rather than executes it is "never blocked and is always responsive to direction." It keeps shared context across months of work, delegates to large numbers of subagents, watches PRs, and can be pointed at a Slack channel or a schedule so it acts on signals it detects without being prompted. The coordinator runs on a cloud machine, explicitly decoupling agent throughput from whether the human's machine is on. Rolling out to everyone in beta. Source-quality note: vendor changelog primary; capability claims including the subagent scale figures are company-reported with no independent evaluation, and "beta rolling out" is not "generally available." **Applicable: YES** — YES, and uncomfortably so, because it is our own architecture with the two pieces we do not have. The Construct already runs the maestro pattern — Cowork orchestrates, Hermes executes with credentials, the Pod clones per project (hypercube-agent-team) — and the daily cadence is already signal-driven through launchd. What Cursor shipped and we have not built is (i) A COORDINATOR THAT IS NEVER BLOCKED BECAUSE IT NEVER EXECUTES, and (ii) COORDINATION THAT SURVIVES THE MACHINE BEING OFF. Both are direct reads on live pain. On (i): this report has repeatedly hit "Hermes is busy in another session," which is the exact failure of a coordinator that also holds the hands — b9-site-repair-headless exists as a workaround for a structural problem Cursor solved by separating the two roles. On (ii): the whole stack is pinned to one Mac and one GPU box, which vironbox-node-resilience already names as a pet-not-cattle single point of failure; Cursor just made "cloud-resident coordinator" a table-stakes feature. The strategic read for Keystone, and it is the fourth instance of the same pattern in eight days: a capability we designed and described is now purchasable. AIR sold the per-skill trust verdict, Anthropic sold the per-team skill economy, Wyre sold traceable scopes, Cursor now sells the delegating coordinator. We are not wrong about the architecture. We are slow to instantiate it, and the differentiator is migrating from "we thought of this" to "ours runs the client's business, not the client's repo."

Two constraints are stated up front and matter more than the feature list: US data residency, and NO ZERO DATA RETENTION SUPPORT. A parallel release the same day, GPT-Live-1, brings full-duplex voice at $0.05/minute for the voice layer with reasoning and tool costs additional. The Hacker News thread on the Agents API is a live argument about managed convenience versus control and single-provider dependence. Source-quality note: carried via an aggregator alert, and under this report's own open-the-primary rule the OpenAI announcement itself was NOT opened this fire — the ZDR and residency constraints in particular should be confirmed against OpenAI's documentation before any decision rests on them. **Applicable: MAYBE** — MAYBE, and the "no" half is the more useful half. CONTEXT COMPACTION AS A MANAGED PRIMITIVE is the piece worth watching: this report has renamed its own freshness/compaction failure class at least six times, reconcile-before-building exists entirely because session compaction silently drops the record of completed work, and a vendor now treats compaction as harness infrastructure rather than each agent's private problem. That is a design idea to steal regardless of whether we ever call the API. But the adoption read is NO for anything real, on one line of the release notes: no Zero Data Retention. Bullet 1 just spent a paragraph establishing that we do not yet know where all of our own context goes; adopting a managed harness with no ZDR would make that worse deliberately rather than by omission. And the HN argument — managed convenience versus control and single-provider dependence — is the same question The Construct answered years ago by running its own executor on its own Mac. The answer has not changed; what changed is that the convenient option is now good enough to be tempting, which is exactly when a stated principle earns its keep.
