10. **CORPUS GAP — the ACE paper names the exact failure mode our skill library runs on: ‘context collapse,’ where iterative rewriting erodes detail over time** ([arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618)) — arXiv 2510.04618 (ICLR 2026, v3 revised Mar 29 2026; Zhang, Hu, Upasani et al., Stanford/SambaNova) identifies two named failure modes in context adaptation — modifying an agent’s inputs rather than its weights. BREVITY BIAS: optimization pressure toward concise summaries drops domain insight. CONTEXT COLLAPSE: iterative rewriting erodes details over time, so a file that is repeatedly revised loses the specifics that made it useful. ACE (Agentic Context Engineering) treats contexts as evolving PLAYBOOKS that accumulate, refine and organize strategies through a modular generation / reflection / curation loop, preventing collapse via structured INCREMENTAL updates rather than rewrites. Reported: +10.6% on agent benchmarks and +8.6% on finance, with lower adaptation latency and rollout cost, adapting from natural execution feedback with no labeled supervision; on the AppWorld leaderboard it matches the top production agent on overall average and beats it on the harder test-challenge split using a smaller open-source model. **Applicable: YES** — YES — and logged honestly as a corpus gap, not as news: the paper is ten months old, was revised in March, and this report has cited its neighbours (2603.09619 nine times, 2607.00151 yesterday) while never once containing the phrases ‘context collapse’ or ‘brevity bias.’ That is the same failure the morning re-scan diagnosed twice — searching for news of the thing instead of the thing — and it matters here because the paper describes our actual operating procedure. The self-improving-loop skill takes a correction from Bobby and revises a skill file; that is iterative rewriting of a context, which is precisely the mechanism ACE names as collapse. The evidence is in this very corpus: the ‘no signage MCP server’ negative survived weeks of rewriting because each pass carried the compressed claim forward instead of the evidence behind it, and by the time it was corrected nobody could see it had never been checked. ACE’s prescription is structural and cheap to adopt — append incrementally with provenance, curate as a separate step, never rewrite wholesale — which is a concrete revision to how the self-improving-loop writes and to how these daily files carry standing claims. Pair it with the four-move rule from bullet 9: a claim re-asserted without re-verification should be a ‘say nothing’ rather than a restatement.
